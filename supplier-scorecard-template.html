Supplier scorecard template

By Michael Olson, Senior Strategic Buyer, 15+ years in manufacturing purchasing.

Short answer: A scorecard grades each supplier on the same criteria, with the same weights, every period. Use five criteria: quality, delivery, cost, service, and terms and risk. Score each one from 1 to 5. Multiply by the weight. Add the results. The total is the grade. A simple card that you use beats a complex card that you skip.

The criteria and weights

CriterionWeightWhat you measure
Quality30%Defects, rework, and rejected parts per delivery period.
Delivery25%On-time rate against the promised date, and lead time stability.
Cost20%Price against market, price stability, and cost-down support.
Service15%Response time, problem handling, and accuracy of paperwork.
Terms and risk10%Payment terms, financial health, and backup capacity.

The weights add to 100%. Quality and delivery carry the most weight. A cheap part that arrives late or fails inspection is not cheap. Adjust the weights to your shop, but fix them before the period starts. Do not change weights after you see the scores.

How to score

Score each criterion from 1 to 5.

  • 5 — Meets the target with room to spare.
  • 4 — Meets the target.
  • 3 — Misses the target in a small way. No harm to production.
  • 2 — Misses the target. Causes extra work, delay, or cost.
  • 1 — Fails. Stops production, scraps parts, or breaks a promise.

Set a numeric target for each criterion before you score. Example targets: defects under 0.5% of parts received scores 4 or more; on-time rate of 95% or more scores 4 or more. A target in numbers stops score drift and stops arguments.

Worked example

CriterionWeightScoreWeighted
Quality30%41.20
Delivery25%30.75
Cost20%51.00
Service15%40.60
Terms and risk10%30.30
Total100%3.85 of 5

Read the card by criterion, not only by total. This supplier has a good total and a weak delivery score. The action is clear: fix delivery first. The total tells you the rank. The lines tell you the work.

Rules for fair use

  • Score on a fixed period. Quarterly works for most shops. Use data from that period only.
  • Use records, not memory. Receiving logs, inspection results, and invoice data. One bad day should not set the grade.
  • Share the card with the supplier. A grade the supplier never sees changes nothing. Send the card, name the weak line, and agree on the fix.
  • Act on the result. Top suppliers get more work. Weak suppliers get a fix plan with a date. A card with no action is paperwork.
