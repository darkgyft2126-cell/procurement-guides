Safety stock calculator

By Michael Olson, Senior Strategic Buyer, 15+ years in manufacturing purchasing.

Short answer: Safety stock is the buffer you keep for late deliveries and high-use days. The simple formula is: (maximum daily use × maximum lead time) − (average daily use × average lead time). For most shop parts, this method is enough. You do not need statistics software.

The formula

Safety stock = (Max daily use × Max lead time in days) − (Avg daily use × Avg lead time in days)

Use your own records. Take daily use from the last 6 to 12 months. Take lead time from real receipts, not from the quote. The quote gives the promised lead time. The dock gives the true lead time.

Worked example

A shop uses a steel flat bar every day.

InputValue
Average daily use50 pieces
Maximum daily use70 pieces
Average lead time10 days
Maximum lead time14 days

Step 1: 70 × 14 = 980. This is the worst case: high use and a late delivery.

Step 2: 50 × 10 = 500. This is the normal case.

Step 3: 980 − 500 = 480 pieces of safety stock.

That buffer covers the gap between a normal week and a bad week. It does not cover a stopped mill or a lost shipment. No buffer covers every case. Set the buffer for the bad week, not for the disaster.

Common mistakes

  • Use the quoted lead time. Suppliers quote the best case. Use the longest real lead time from your receipts.
  • Set one rule for all parts. A cheap bolt and a cast housing need different buffers. Set safety stock per part, or per part group.
  • Never review the number. Use patterns change. Review safety stock at least once a year, and after any supplier change.
  • Count stock you cannot use. Damaged, rusty, or wrong-revision stock is not a buffer. Count only good stock.
  • Set the buffer by fear. One stock-out hurts. But a buffer set too high ties up cash on the shelf for years. Use the formula, not the memory of one bad day.
