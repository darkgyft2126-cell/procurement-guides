Landed cost calculator

By Michael Olson, Senior Strategic Buyer, 15+ years in manufacturing purchasing.

Short answer: Landed cost is the full cost of a part at your door. It is the goods price plus freight, insurance, duty, broker fees, and local delivery. Compare suppliers on landed cost, not on the goods price. A low goods price with high freight and duty can cost more than a high goods price with freight included.

The cost elements

Landed cost = Goods + Freight + Insurance + Duty + Broker and port fees + Local delivery

  • Goods. The invoice price of the parts.
  • Freight. Every leg: factory to port, port to port, port to your door. Include fuel surcharges.
  • Insurance. Cargo insurance for the shipment. Small cost. Large protection.
  • Duty. The import tax. It is a percent of the customs value. The rate depends on the part type and the country of origin. Check the current rate before you quote, not after.
  • Broker and port fees. Customs broker fee, port handling, and exam fees if customs inspects the load.
  • Local delivery. The last leg to your dock, plus any storage or demurrage if the load waits.

Worked example

A shop imports 1,000 machined parts.

ElementCost
Goods (EXW price)$10,000
Freight, all legs$1,600
Cargo insurance$45
Duty at 2.5% of goods value$250
Broker and port fees$175
Local delivery$230
Total landed cost$12,300

Landed cost per part: $12,300 ÷ 1,000 = $12.30 per part.

The goods price was $10.00 per part. The true cost is 23% more. If you set your sell price or your budget from $10.00, the error comes out of your margin.

Why EXW quotes mislead

EXW means Ex Works. The price covers the goods at the seller's door. You pay for every step after that door. You also carry the risk for every step after that door.

  • The number looks low. It leaves out freight, duty, and fees. In the example, it leaves out $2,300 on a $10,000 order.
  • Two EXW quotes are hard to compare. Two factories in two countries give two EXW prices. The freight and duty differ by country. The low EXW price can give the high landed cost.
  • The risk sits with you early. Under EXW, the goods are your problem from the seller's dock. If export paperwork fails, the delay is yours.

The fix is simple. Ask each supplier for a price on the same terms. DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) puts freight and duty in the seller's price. Or ask for EXW and add your own landed cost with the method on this page. Compare like with like. Never compare one supplier's EXW price to another supplier's DDP price.
